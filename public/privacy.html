Privacy policy

Effective 16 October 2026. Applies to the Trait5 website and iPhone app.

In short:

  • There is no account and no Trait5 server. Your answers, goals, plans and check-ins stay on your device.
  • The app counts anonymous usage (which screens are opened and milestones such as a check-in), with no cookies. You can turn this off in Settings.
  • Nothing is sold, there is no advertising, and nothing tracks you across apps or websites.

What the app stores, and where

Everything you enter (questionnaire answers and scores, goals and the reasons you give for them, if-then plans, what you log each day, mood check-ins, weekly reflections and ratings from friends) is stored only on your device:

  • Website: in your browser's storage for this site. Clearing your browser's data for the site erases it.
  • iPhone app: inside the app. It is included in your iPhone's backups (iCloud or computer), like other app data. Deleting the app erases it from the phone.

Trait5 has no account and no server that receives or keeps this data. The website and the app keep separate copies, and so do different browsers and devices; nothing syncs between them unless you move it yourself (see below).

The website is hosted on GitHub Pages. Like any web host, GitHub receives standard technical information (such as your IP address and browser type) when your browser loads the site's files; see GitHub's privacy statement. The website also saves its own files in your browser so it works offline. The iPhone app's files are built into the app.

Anonymous usage counts

To see whether the app is useful, the published website and the iPhone app send anonymous counts to GoatCounter. Copies of the app running anywhere else (for example, a developer's own computer) send nothing. Each count contains only:

  • the name of the screen opened, such as /today, and its title; or
  • the name of a milestone: starting, finishing or retaking the questionnaire, setting a goal, starting a program week, doing a weekly reflection, a check-in, completing a challenge, or adding a friend's rating;
  • the address of the page that linked you to the site, sent once with the first screen, if there was one;
  • a random number that stops the request being cached.

It never includes your answers, scores, goals, plans, check-ins, anything you type, or the contents of a rating or transfer link. No cookies or identifiers are used. Like any web request, the request itself reaches GoatCounter with your IP address and browser details, which GoatCounter uses to produce totals such as browser type and country; see GoatCounter's privacy policy.

Counts start when the app opens. To stop them, turn off Share anonymous usage counts in Settings → Your data. That choice is remembered on your device. People who open a rating link you send are counted the same way on the website, by screen name only.

Daily reminders

  • iPhone app: "Remind me daily" asks for permission to show notifications, then schedules a daily notification on the phone itself. No server is involved, and you can turn it off in the iPhone's Settings → Notifications.
  • Website: "Add to calendar" makes a calendar file on your device for you to open. It includes your goals and the reasons you gave for them. If you add it to an online calendar (such as Google, iCloud or Outlook), that service stores it under its own privacy policy.

Friend ratings

If you ask someone to rate you, the app makes a link containing the first name you chose to show (if any), which traits to rate, and a random code. The private label you give that person stays on your device. You send the link yourself, through whatever you choose (your messaging app, email or the share sheet).

Your friend opens it on the Trait5 website and answers in their own browser. Their answers are not saved there or sent to any Trait5 server: they are packed into a reply link (with the relationship they picked and the date) that they send back to you the same way. Adding it saves the rating on your device.

The rating details are in the part of the link after the #, which browsers don't send to the website's host. The messaging service you use to send a link handles it under its own policy.

Moving your data, backups and messages you share

  • Move your data: a transfer code is all of your data, compressed into text on your device. You copy it and paste it into another browser or the app. It doesn't pass through any server, but anyone who has the code can read your data, so treat it like the data itself.
  • Export backup (website): saves a file with all of your data to your device. Where you keep it is up to you. Importing a backup replaces the data on that device. The iPhone app uses the transfer code instead.
  • Someone who checks in: the follow-through kit can copy or share a short message with your goals and this week's plans. You choose whether and to whom to send it.

The app links to outside sites (research papers, helplines). If you open one, that site's own privacy policy applies; Trait5 sends it nothing about you.

The AI coach (not enabled in this version)

The current version of Trait5 has no AI features and sends nothing to any AI service. A future version may offer an optional AI coach. If it does, nothing would be sent unless you tap its button, and the app would say what is sent, right next to that button, before you do. It would send:

  • Help me write this plan: your goal, the chosen challenge, your current plan and the situation you type.
  • Review my week: that week's plans and targets, what you logged each day, how much you enjoyed each plan, your weekly reflection, and the problem, aim and barrier you gave for that goal.

This would go to Trait5's coach server and to Anthropic, whose Claude model writes the suggestions. Trait5's server wouldn't keep what you sent: it keeps only daily request and cost counts, under a scrambled (hashed) network key, to limit abuse and cost. Anthropic deletes API inputs and outputs within 30 days (longer, up to 2 years, only if its safety systems flag them) and doesn't use them to train its models; see Anthropic's privacy center. This policy would be updated before such a feature is turned on.

What Trait5 doesn't do

  • It doesn't sell or rent data, or share it with data brokers.
  • It has no advertising and no advertising identifiers.
  • It doesn't track you across other apps or websites.
  • It doesn't need an account, email address, phone number or location. For friend ratings it asks for an optional first name to show, which goes only in the link you send, and a label for that person, which stays on your device.

App Store

If you install the iPhone app, Apple handles your App Store account and purchase under its own privacy policy. Apple may give developers aggregated statistics, such as downloads, and crash reports and usage data from people who chose to share them with app developers in their iPhone's settings.

Children

Trait5 is not directed at children under 13, and it doesn't knowingly collect personal information from anyone. It is a self-improvement tool, not therapy or a diagnosis.

Your choices and rights

Because your data is held only on your device, you control it directly, and there is nothing on a Trait Trainer server to request, correct or delete:

  • See it in the app. To get a copy of all of it, use Settings → Export backup on the website, or copy a transfer code in Settings → Move your data.
  • Delete it with Settings → Delete all data, by clearing the site's data in your browser, or by deleting the app (and, if you want, old iPhone backups).
  • Stop the anonymous usage counts with the switch in Settings. The counts are not linked to you, so they can't be looked up or deleted for one person.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new effective date. Changes that send more data off your device, such as turning on the AI coach, will be explained in the app before they take effect.
