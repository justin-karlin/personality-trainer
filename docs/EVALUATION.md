# Evaluating Trait Trainer

Trait Trainer applies strategies with research behind them, but the app itself has not been tested. This plan sets out how to test it, in two stages, without overstating what each stage can show. It follows recommendation 11 of the October 2026 evidence review.

The evaluation has to tell three claims apart:

1. **People used the app.** Usage counts and retention show this, and nothing more.
2. **People improved.** Before-and-after change in users shows this. It can't show the app caused the change: expectations, self-selection, regression to the mean and life events all produce change too (Krämer et al. 2025 found waitlist groups improved almost as much as program groups).
3. **The app improved outcomes more than a credible alternative.** Only a randomized comparison shows this.

## Stage 1: usability and feasibility

Goal: find out whether people can use the core loop. That loop is to choose a real problem, name the barrier, make a cue–action plan, act, log what happened, and review.

- **Moderated sessions** with 5–8 people per round, using think-aloud on their own phones. Watch for where people stall: naming a problem, picking a barrier, writing the plan, logging a "no chance" day, using the reflection.
- **Ask people who stop using it why.** Use a short optional question, or a follow-up message from the researcher. Return rates alone don't explain drop-off.
- **Onboarding comparison:** questionnaire-first (current) against a quick start that begins with the follow-through problem. Measure completion of the first week's plan and first log. Keep the full IPIP-50 for anyone whose trait change will be studied, and give it before they see any program content.
- **Report usability and retention separately from any claim about benefit.**

The app's anonymous GoatCounter counts (screens and milestone names) are enough for stage 1 funnels. They are not research data and must not be expanded quietly.

## Stage 2: a prespecified outcome study

**Design.** Randomize consenting adults who want to stop putting things off to one of two arms for the same 12 weeks:
- Trait Trainer's focused follow-through pathway: problem, barrier, cue–action plans, action log and weekly review.
- A credible basic alternative: a planner with reminders and a task list, with matched contact and notifications.

**Primary outcome, chosen in advance.** One of:
- a validated procrastination scale (for example the Pure Procrastination Scale or the Irrational Procrastination Scale);
- a well-defined behavioural outcome with defensible checking (for example, the share of planned task starts that happened on the planned day, confirmed by a timestamped artefact).

**Secondary outcomes,** kept distinct:
- task initiation and completion;
- impairment from procrastination;
- retention;
- IPIP-50 conscientiousness, with observer ratings where available. Treat trait change as secondary unless a separately powered trait study is the explicit aim.

**Unwanted effects.** Track increased guilt or self-criticism, interpersonal strain (especially for getting-along exercises) and dropout because of distress. The weekly reflection's optional "Did anything backfire?" question gives qualitative signal. The study should also ask directly.

**Follow-up.** Post-program and at least 3 months later. Collect outcomes from people who stopped using the app too, and analyse everyone as randomized (intention to treat), not only completers.

**Sample size.** Base it on the smallest between-group difference that would matter to users, and on expected missing data. Don't use a convenient round number. Procrastination treatments compared with inactive controls averaged g ≈ 0.34 (Rozental et al. 2018). The difference from an active planner will likely be smaller, so the trial may need several hundred people per arm. Run a formal power analysis before recruiting.

**Prespecification.** Register the hypotheses, primary outcome, analysis model, missing-data handling (for example multiple imputation under missing at random, with sensitivity analyses) and stopping rules before seeing comparative results.

## Consent and data

- Research participation needs **explicit, separate consent**. Ordinary app use never enrols anyone.
- Research data goes through a **separate, pseudonymous pathway** that people opt into: for example an export they choose to send, tagged with a study ID. The app's local-only storage and anonymous counts stay as they are.
- Participants can withdraw and have their study data deleted.
- Get ethics review (an IRB or equivalent) before stage 2.

## What not to conclude

- Rising check-in ratings, streaks or completion counts are not evidence of personality change.
- Pre–post improvement among users is not evidence that the app works.
- Improvement on one outcome (for example task starts) does not imply improvement on another (for example agreeableness).
- One favourable friend rating is not evidence of change. Change needs the same observer before and after.
